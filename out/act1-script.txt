Start with the map. Because the map is where the whole thing falls apart. The 2015 survey was led by an anthropologist named William Jankowiak. It's the largest study of its kind, and romantic kissing showed up in less than half the cultures on record. And it was least common in the small foraging groups, the people living closest to how humans lived for most of our history. That is the opposite of what you'd expect from an instinct. Some cultures greet with the nose instead. The Inuit kunik isn't a kiss. You press your nose to someone's skin, and you breathe in. You're not tasting them. You're smelling them. Other cultures press their foreheads together. Some just sniff a cheek. All over the world, humans reach for each other's faces. But the mouth is not the default. It's just one option among many. So kissing looks cultural. A habit some places invented, and others simply skipped. A habit. Not an instinct. Except that isn't the whole picture either.
